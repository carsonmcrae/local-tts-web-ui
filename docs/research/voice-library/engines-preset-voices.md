# Preset narrator voices for TTSNative: engine survey

Read-only web research. Every fact below was read on **2026-10-21** unless a page itself states another date. Labels: **documented** (page says it; URL given), **claimed** (the author's own claim or the author's own benchmark), **inference** (my reasoning), **secondary** (a third-party summary of a page I could not open). Licence sentences are quoted verbatim where the page gave them. "Not documented" means the pages read today are silent. This is a research note, not legal advice.

Scope: the maintainer's 2026-10-21 cloning comparison is taken as given (F5 CC-BY-NC weights, XTTS CPML, Fish community licence, Spark/MaskGCT NC, IndexTTS2 bilibili, Higgs, VibeVoice withdrawn, MegaTTS3, CosyVoice Apache, VoxCPM2, dots.tts, Qwen3-TTS, GPT-SoVITS, Orpheus/Sesame Llama, Dia, Zonos, Piper, Pocket TTS, NeuTTS, AA Elo figures). ## 1. Answer

On today's reading, **no engine on the focus list meets all three of (a) rated more natural than Kokoro by independent listening data, (b) permissive code and weights with no non-commercial, research-only or revenue-capped terms, and (c) CPU near real time.** The only independent listening data that includes Kokoro is the Artificial Analysis arena (documented, https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/arena?tab=leaderboard): Kokoro 82M v1.0 sits at Elo 1065 (rank 57 of 98, 5,427 samples) and is _above_ every other permissively licensed preset-voice engine listed there (Chatterbox 1024, Zonos 1000, OpenVoice v2 956, XTTS v2 916, StyleTTS 2 892); NVIDIA Magpie-Multilingual 357M ties it at 1063. The open-weights models above Kokoro are either non-commercial (Fish Drama 3 Preview 1248 community licence; Breeze TTS 2 1225 "research and non-commercial use only"; Voxtral TTS 1091 CC-BY-NC-4.0) or GPU-class: **Ming-3.0-tts-0.5B (MIT, Elo 1190, "100+ Chinese and five English" built-in voices, 44.1 kHz, tested only on H800/H20, no CPU statement)** and Step-Audio-EditX (cloning only, weights licence not stated). Pocket TTS, Qwen3-TTS (open weights), Supertonic, KittenTTS, MeloTTS, Parler, Orpheus, Dia2, NeuTTS, OuteTTS and CosyVoice 3 do not appear on that leaderboard; the two "Qwen3 TTS" entries that do appear (930 and 943, probably the hosted API, not confirmed) are below Kokoro; Kyutai's own test (secondary) rated Pocket TTS audio-quality Elo 2016 below Chatterbox Turbo 2055 and did not rate Kokoro. The HF TTS Arena V2 leaderboard could not be retrieved today (JavaScript Space; docs give no snapshot). **Practical options:** for an optional GPU "high quality" mode with presets, Ming-3.0-tts-0.5B (MIT) is the only engine with independent evidence of beating Kokoro, followed by Qwen3-TTS 1.7B-CustomVoice (Apache-2.0, 9 presets, measured ~4 GB VRAM for the 0.6B per one blog, not rated above Kokoro) and Magpie 357M (NVIDIA Open Model License, commercial use allowed with guardrail and litigation clauses; NeMo is Linux-only, a C++ GGUF port exists). For CPU near real time with presets, Pocket TTS (MIT code, CC-BY-4.0 weights, 26 presets, ~6x real time claimed on an M4 Air) is the strongest candidate but has no independent evidence of beating Kokoro; Supertonic 3 is fast (independent RTF 0.24 at 5 steps vs Kokoro 0.64 on a 4-core Xeon) but its weights are OpenRAIL-M with use restrictions including a mandatory machine-generated disclaimer, and the repository is archived; KittenTTS 2 weights carry a USD 1M revenue cap; Chatterbox Nano is MIT but has no named presets. **Provenance of the humans behind preset voices** is documented only for: Pocket TTS (per-voice sources in kyutai/tts-voices: VCTK CC-BY-4.0 speakers, LibriVox narrators via Voice-Zero CC0, CC0 volunteer donations, voice actor Alba MacKenna CC-BY-4.0, plus two presets from CC-BY-NC datasets), Magpie (John Van Stan is a LibriVox reader; the other four are "proprietary"), Kani-TTS (12 source datasets listed), Parler (LibriTTS-R / MLS English audiobook datasets, name-to-speaker mapping undocumented) and Kokoro only for af_sky (hexgrad: "about 3 minutes of 2023 Sky" from OpenAI-hosted clips); every other engine is silent.

## 3. Per-engine notes

### 3.1 Kokoro-82M (hexgrad)

- Repo: https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M (weights, VOICES.md); code https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro (9.6k stars, 71 commits, last commit 2025-12-05 "pypi mismatch" fix; documented); ONNX port https://github.com/thewh1teagle/kokoro-onnx (2.8k stars, version 0.6.1, last commit 2026-09-01; documented). v1.0 published "2025 Jan 27"; v0.19 "2024 Dec 25" (model card release table; documented).
- Licence (documented, model card): front matter `license: apache-2.0`; "With Apache-licensed weights, Kokoro can be deployed anywhere from production environments to personal projects." and "This is an Apache-licensed model, and Kokoro has been deployed in numerous projects and commercial APIs." Code Apache-2.0; kokoro-onnx MIT. v0.19 card noted a "GPLv3 dependency in espeak-ng" (documented, https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/kLegacy/blob/main/v0.19/README.md).
- Use terms: none beyond the licence (documented). Warning about unaffiliated "kokoro" domains only.
- Training data, verbatim (documented, https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/raw/main/README.md): "Kokoro was trained exclusively on **permissive/non-copyrighted audio data** and IPA phoneme labels." Listed examples: "Public domain audio"; "Audio licensed under Apache, MIT, etc"; "Synthetic audio" "generated by closed" "TTS models from large providers"; exclusion: "No synthetic audio from open TTS models or "custom voice clones"". "Total Dataset Size: A few hundred hours of audio." "Total Training Cost: About $1000 for 1000 hours of A100 80GB vRAM". Acknowledgement: "Thank you to everyone who contributed synthetic training data." CC-BY sources named: Koniwa (CC BY 3.0), SIWIS (CC BY 4.0).
- Architecture (documented): StyleTTS 2 + ISTFTNet, "Decoder only: no diffusion, no encoder release"; 82M params; 24 kHz (usage code `rate=24000`); "Under the hood, `kokoro` uses misaki, a G2P library".
- VOICES.md (documented, https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md): 54 voices, 29F/25M. American English 20, British 8, Japanese 5, Mandarin 8, Spanish 3, French 1, Hindi 4, Italian 2, Brazilian Portuguese 3. Grades: "the given grades are intended to be estimates of the quality and quantity of its associated training data"; Target Quality = audio quality of the reference voice and label fit; Training Duration bands from "1 minute <= M minutes 🤏 < 10 minutes" to "10 hours <= HH hours < 100 hours". Notable: af_heart target A (no overall grade); af_bella A-; af_nicole B-; bf_emma B-; ff_siwis B- (<11 h, SIWIS CC-BY); af_sky "M minutes 🤏", overall C-; most others C/D; am_adam F+. The page says nothing about whether any voice is synthetic or who the speakers are, except CC-BY links for Koniwa (Japanese) and SIWIS (French). Long-form note (documented): voices work best on a "goldilocks range" of 100-200 tokens, rush on long inputs.
- af_sky, hexgrad's own words (documented, https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/ee030eb3e7ab4b8a2f52f1324366cc7429424bf4/demo/restoring-sky.md): "we can cobble together about 3 minutes of 2023 Sky" from five OpenAI-hosted MP3s plus "a few seconds lying around various other parts of the internet"; Sky is "the voice that ScarJo [got OpenAI to take down]" so "new training data cannot be generated"; af_sky is "maybe a shadow of its former self"; "Tonight on NYE, `af_sky` joins Kokoro's roster of downloadable voices." The v0.19 card lists release dates per voice (af_bella/af_sarah 25 Dec 2024 ... af_nicole 30 Dec, af_sky 31 Dec 2024) and the default `af` as a 50-50 mix of Bella and Sarah; it gives no per-voice data origin and does not name OpenAI (documented).
- Name overlap (observation, not hexgrad's words): OpenAI's TTS docs list 13 built-in voices "alloy, ash, ballad, coral, echo, fable, nova, onyx, sage, shimmer, verse, marin, cedar" (documented, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/text-to-speech). Kokoro v1.0 has af_alloy, am_echo, bm_fable, af_nova, am_onyx (5 of those 13) plus af_sky (OpenAI's withdrawn voice). Gemini's prebuilt voices include Puck, Kore, Fenrir, Aoede (documented, https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation); Kokoro has am_puck, af_kore, am_fenrir, af_aoede. hexgrad's only general statement is the training-data line about "Synthetic audio generated by closed TTS models from large providers"; no per-voice provenance beyond af_sky is documented. Whether the OpenAI/Google-named voices derive from those providers' output is **not documented** (inference: the naming is consistent with that statement, nothing more).
- Size/format: official `.pth` loaded with `torch.load(..., weights_only=True)` (documented, GitHub README); kokoro-onnx "Lightweight: ~300MB (quantized: ~80MB)" (claimed). No trust_remote_code; plain pip packages. Windows: espeak-ng `*.msi` install documented in the kokoro README.
- Speed: kokoro-onnx "Fast performance near real-time on macOS M1" (claimed). Independent CPU: community report, 4-core Xeon Platinum 8272CL, CPU-only ONNX, af_heart, 5 timed runs: mean RTF 0.6415 (1.6x real time), per-length 0.81 (12 chars) to 0.52 (1,712 chars); UTMOS 4.44 (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gauravvij/kokoro-tts-vs-supertonic-3-tts/main/results/benchmark_report.md; author not named; a Reddit mirror says the run was set up by an AI coding agent, so treat as unverified). Supertone's own table: Kokoro CPU ONNX on M4 Pro RTF 0.124-0.144 (vendor, https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-2). A blog cites "~5x" on a 32-core CPU without measuring (claimed, https://ocdevel.com/blog/20260711-quantized-cpu-tts).
- Quality evidence: AA arena Elo 1065, rank 57/98, 5,427 samples (documented). Kyutai's Pocket TTS post reports Kokoro WER 1.93 on LibriSpeech test-clean and that Kokoro ran real time on Intel Core Ultra 7 165H / Apple M3 (secondary via https://news.hada.io/topic?id=25871; the blog body at https://kyutai.org/blog/2026-01-13-pocket-tts did not render for me). A hosted-TTS blog author calls Kokoro "terrible TTS quality, but blazing fast" (claimed, informal).
- Pronunciation/normalisation: see section 4.

### 3.2 Chatterbox Turbo, Multilingual V3, Nano (Resemble AI)

- Repo: https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox (27.1k stars; last commits 2026-07-21 "Update ChatterboxNano (#542)", 2026-06-10 "v3 multilingual and single language pack release"; documented). MIT code; HF `ResembleAI/chatterbox-turbo` and `ResembleAI/chatterbox-nano` tagged `License: mit` (documented). An HF repo named chatterbox-multilingual-v3 returned 401; the org page lists per-language packs (hi, pt-pt, es-es, pt-br, es-mx-latam, zh-cmn) and a Space "Chatterbox-Multilingual-TTS-V3"; V3 is described in the GitHub README as 500M, 23+ languages (documented).
- Variants (documented README): Turbo 350M English, paralinguistic tags; Nano 110M English, "3x faster than realtime on 8-core CPU" (claimed); Multilingual V3 500M; six single-language 500M finetunes; original 500M.
- Presets: no named preset voices. The Turbo repo ships `conds.pt` (169 kB, pickle, 4 flagged imports; documented file listing). `tts_turbo.py` loads `ckpt_dir / "conds.pt"` as built-in conditionals if present, so `generate()` works without `audio_prompt_path`; otherwise it asserts "Please `prepare_conditionals` first or specify `audio_prompt_path`" (documented, raw source). Provenance of that default voice: README "Prompts are sourced from freely available data on the internet." (documented). Nothing more.
- Use terms (documented): "Every audio file generated by Chatterbox includes" Resemble's Perth watermark; "Don't use this model to do bad things." Turbo repo total 4.04 GB (t3_turbo_v1.safetensors 1.92 GB, s3gen 1.06 GB, s3gen_meanflow 1.06 GB, ve 5.7 MB). Sample rate `S3GEN_SR = 24000` (documented).
- Quality: AA Chatterbox 1024 (open), Chatterbox HD 1109 (hosted). Kyutai's test: Chatterbox Turbo audio-quality Elo 2055 ± 23, highest of the five systems, WER 3.24 (secondary). No Chatterbox-specific long-text issue surfaced in one search (a mirror shows a repetition-penalty default of 1.2; secondary).
- Windows: not mentioned ("developed and tested on Python 3.11 on Debian 11").

### 3.3 Qwen3-TTS CustomVoice / VoiceDesign (0.6B, 1.7B)

- Repo: https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS (13.9k stars, Apache-2.0, series announced 2026-01-22, last commit 2026-03-17; documented). HF https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-CustomVoice `License: apache-2.0`, safetensors BF16, metadata "2B params"; 0.6B-CustomVoice also Apache (documented).
- Presets (documented): "supports 9 premium timbres covering various combinations of gender, age, language, and dialect": Vivian, Serena, Uncle_Fu, Dylan (Beijing), Eric (Chengdu) for Chinese; Ryan, Aiden for English; Ono_Anna (Japanese); Sohee (Korean). Each can speak any supported language. Provenance/consent: **not documented**. 1.7B adds natural-language `instruct` style control; VoiceDesign builds voices from text descriptions.
- Use terms: none beyond Apache; no watermark mentioned (documented).
- Size/format: safetensors; `qwen-tts` package (Transformers), flash-attn optional, vLLM-Omni offline; conda Python 3.12; sample rate not stated on card or README (tokenizer is 12.5 Hz frames) (documented). Windows not mentioned; community forks publish Windows steps with prebuilt flash-attn wheels and `triton-windows` (secondary).
- Speed: "end-to-end synthesis latency as low as 97ms" (claimed). CPU, independent: on a 4-vCPU/15 GB box, 0.6B int8 RTF 2.85-3.17 ("seconds of compute per second of audio"), i.e. about 0.33x real time; int4 slower than int8 (https://ocdevel.com/blog/20260711-quantized-cpu-tts, July 2026). The official FAQ mirror says the 0.6B is the practical CPU choice and CPU is 10-50x slower than GPU (secondary). VRAM: ~4 GB measured for 0.6B on an RTX 4080 SUPER (claimed, Medium); 4-6 GB (guide, secondary).
- Quality: AA lists "Qwen3 TTS" (Jan 2026) Elo 930 and "Qwen3 TTS Flash" 943, both below Kokoro; whether these are the open weights or the Alibaba Cloud API is not stated. Author eval: CustomVoice WER 0.903 zh / 0.899 en; long-zh 2.356, long-en 2.812; InstructTTSEval scores; no MOS (claimed). ## 4. The Kokoro phonemiser question (misaki vs espeak-ng)

- What v0.19 was trained with: the v0.19 card's usage code installs `phonemizer` and `espeak-ng`, and its limitations say "Relies on external g2p (espeak-ng), which introduces a class of g2p failure modes." (documented, https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/kLegacy/blob/main/v0.19/README.md).
- What v1.0 was trained with, hexgrad's words (documented, https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro/issues/103, 2025-02-17): "As of v1.0, the Kokoro model is trained on the Misaki G2P engine." "Minor deviations from Misaki may be unnoticeable or tolerable, but in general you diverge on G2P at your own risk." "Crossing phonemizers is not recommended." "Adhering to standard IPA is an afterthought, and indeed in v1.0 this adherence is broken." Mechanism: misaki emits diphthongs as single tokens (e.g. `ɔɪ` -> `Y`, misaki output `ðə nˈYz ɪz jˈuzd tʊ ənˈY.`), espeak emits two symbols (`ɔ ɪ`); the reporter's "noise" mispronunciation came from espeak input and he judged espeak plus lexicon fixes otherwise "very good" (anecdotal, same issue). The kokoro README adds that espeak is "used for English OOD fallback and some non-English languages" (documented).
- What misaki requires (documented, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hexgrad/misaki/main/pyproject.toml and PyPI): version 0.9.4 (2025-04-05); Apache-2.0; Python >=3.8,<3.14; core deps addict, pip>=25.0.1, regex; extra `[en]` = num2words, spacy, spacy-curated-transformers, phonemizer-fork, espeakng-loader, torch, transformers; `[ja]` = fugashi, jaconv, mojimoji, unidic, pyopenjtalk; `[zh]` = jieba, ordered-set, pypinyin, cn2an, pypinyin-dict; `[ko]` = jamo, nltk; `[vi]` = num2words, spacy, spacy-curated-transformers, underthesea; `[he]` = mishkal-hebrew. README: "Misaki is a G2P engine designed for Kokoro models."; default `en.G2P(trf=False, british=False, fallback=None)`; espeak fallback needs system espeak-ng plus phonemizer-fork ("Now outofdictionary words are handled by espeak."); "Installing espeak varies across platforms." No spaCy model name (en_core_web_sm) is listed in the extras.
- What kokoro-onnx does (documented): pyproject deps onnxruntime>=1.20.1, espeakng-loader>=0.2.4, phonemizer>=3.4.0, numpy>=2.0.2 (plain `phonemizer`, not the fork). `src/kokoro_onnx/tokenizer.py` phonemizes with phonemizer's espeak backend (bundled library via espeakng_loader, env override `PHONEMIZER_ESPEAK_LIBRARY`, system fallback), `preserve_punctuation=True`, `with_stress=True`, languages `en-us` / `en-gb`, a global lock because "espeak-ng holds process-global state", and `normalize_text` only strips whitespace: no number, date or currency expansion. README: "Note: It's recommend to use misaki g2p package from v1.0, see examples"; the visible examples list (28 files) has `with_phonemes.py` (pre-phonemised input) but no `with_misaki.py` was visible today (listing truncated; unresolved). Inference: TTSNative can feed misaki phonemes through the pre-phonemised path without changing the ONNX runtime.
- Reported quality differences (all anecdotal, none controlled): Mandarin: espeak drops tones; an ASR check recovered 41% of characters from espeak's reading vs 97% from misaki's on 36 sentences (langx PR #1582, secondary). English listening: one project judged misaki+phonemizer best ("Tchaikovsky" misaki `ʧˌaɪkˈɑfski` vs espeak `tˈiːtʃeɪkˈɑːvski`) (slicc-ortllama PR #49, secondary). Diphthong tokens: issue #103 above. Coverage: misaki has no Maori (a blog used espeak instead) and no pt-BR (kokoro-onnx issue #108: pt-BR via espeak "almost perfect"; the maintainer kept pointing to misaki without a verdict) (documented/secondary). Number handling: misaki[en] uses num2words; kokoro-onnx does none (documented); Supertonic and KittenTTS 2 document built-in number/currency/date expansion, Pocket TTS does not document its normaliser. No MOS or arena comparison of misaki vs espeak for Kokoro was found.
